# Simplify and Jobright: documented capabilities

Research date: **2026-10-28**. Scope: official public product pages, help articles, and privacy policies. These establish what the vendors document or market; they do **not** independently verify reliability, matching quality, interview lift, or the live paid experience. User complaints are intentionally outside this source set and require separate evidence.

## Pricing and verification limits

Simplify's current official plan guide lists **$19.99/week, $39.99/month, and $89.99/three months** for Simplify+. The same page presents paid AI tailoring, writing, networking, and application insights. This is a dated observation, not a permanent price guarantee. [Official pricing](https://help.simplify.jobs/articles/5623502-whats-included-in-simplify-features-and-pricing)

I could not verify a current public **job-seeker** Jobright subscription price in the inspected official pages. Its homepage's Pricing link currently resolves to an **employer recruiting** plan, so the displayed $499/recruiter/month must not be quoted as the job-seeker price. Free-start marketing also does not establish unlimited free AI usage. [Employer pricing](https://jobright.ai/employers/pricing)

## Privacy and control deserve product design, not a slogan

Simplify's August 19, 2026 policy allows selected recruiter matches to receive relevant candidate information even without a separate application. It also permits using profile and interaction information to improve its own AI systems, with specific restrictions for connected email, extension information, and voluntary self-identification. Gmail storage is described as metadata and short previews, with full messages fetched when used. It provides account/privacy controls. Summarizing this merely as “nothing shared unless you apply” would omit material policy detail. [Privacy policy](https://simplify.jobs/privacy)

Jobright's June 15, 2026 policy describes professional-data processing, third-party AI processors, recruiter/employer use, rights requests, and encrypted Gmail tokens deleted when disconnected. Its Gmail purpose is narrowly described above; do not infer broader email reading from the availability of an application agent. [Privacy policy](https://jobright.ai/legal/privacy)

**Inference for Resume Butler:** show what an integration reads, what is retained, which provider receives a resume or email excerpt, and how users disconnect/delete/export. Keep saved demographic and authorization answers explicitly user-controlled. Do not silently turn a private job-search workspace into a recruiter database.

## Product conclusions supported by this capability scan

1. **Feature parity is already broad.** Basic tailoring, a match score, tracking, and autofill are not distinctive by themselves.
2. **Continuity is a promising wedge.** The same selected resume should drive scoring, form filling, the actual submitted PDF, and the application record. A wrong-version upload can erase the value of good tailoring.
3. **Traceable fit is more credible than a high score.** Show the job requirement, the user's supporting evidence, and any missing or uncertain fact separately.
4. **The tracker should lead to action.** A record with the next follow-up, interview context, original posting, and submission proof is more useful than a status board alone.
5. **Deep reliability beats speculative breadth for a personal CS-first tool.** Build and measure the reviewed workflow before attempting a job index or fully automatic submissions.

These conclusions are product hypotheses from documented capability overlap. They need to be combined with user reports and direct workflow testing before claiming unmet demand or competitor failure.
